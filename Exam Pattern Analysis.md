9. **She skipped**: numbers and statistics, historical people, disease tables, origin hypotheses, and phylogeny trees. These are *lower priority*, not *safe*. See §7. **Human uses were tested once** (Q32: red algae are the source of agar and carrageenan); most other uses were skipped or used as wrong answers.
10. **The exam is shallow per question but broad.** Each question tests one fact, and no question needed multi-step reasoning or math (she also said scratch paper is never needed).
11. **Study the PowerPoints first and use the textbook as a targeted reference**: for image-only slides with no text alternative, terms you can't explain, and slides that look wrong. When a slide and the textbook disagree, follow the textbook (her Oct 22 email). See §5.3 and §10.

## 8. Distractor patterns (useful for fast elimination)

- **Cross-chapter vocabulary**: "peptidoglycan" appeared in wrong answers about animals, fungi and plants; "conjugation pilus" in wrong answers about fungi and Euglena; "basidiospores/asci" in wrong answers about protists. If an option drops a term into an organism that can't have it, eliminate it.
- **Absolute words**: "only," "all," "always," "exclusively," "must" and "every" were wrong every time (22 wrong choices, zero correct answers).
- **Kingdom mismatches**: "the bacteria must belong to Archaea," "zoospore is produced only by bacteria," "their cells are prokaryotic" (for oomycetes).
- **The one plausible distractor is almost always the slide-mate**: the other term from the same slide or list (transduction vs. transformation, micronucleus vs. macronucleus, stolons vs. rhizoids, asci vs. basidia, plasmodial vs. cellular slime molds, brown vs. red algae). **Your notes should put these pairs side by side.**

## 9. Open-note tactics

1. **Keep notes in slide order, with a tab per chapter and a clear heading per section** (use the PowerPoint's section numbers; see the section map in §10). Questions follow the deck chapter by chapter and section by section, so you can flip forward through your notes as you go instead of searching.
2. **Have a "look-alike terms" table per chapter** (the printed notes include one): two- or three-column contrasts of the terms that get confused (the study guide lists them for each Unit 2 chapter). This is where points are lost.
3. **Have organism → feature rows in one place** (the printed notes' term index gives each organism's group and defining feature). Three questions on Unit 1 were "pairing" questions built from rows like these.
4. **For every life cycle, know which stages are haploid (n) or diploid (2n), and where meiosis and fertilization happen** (the printed notes' n/2n/3n list and life-cycle table). Unit 2 is life-cycle heavy, and the one diagram-dependent question on Unit 1 (Q35) asked exactly this.
5. **Read the "Text Alternative" slides at the end of each deck.** They are written-out versions of the diagrams, and that's where the Ulva answer lived. In Ch 23, the moss, fern and pine life-cycle steps exist **only** as image slides plus their text alternatives (moss: slides 100–102; fern: 105–107; pine: 108–112). The flowering-plant cycle also has bullet slides (86–88).
6. **For image-only slides with no text alternative, read the textbook's caption for that figure.** The slide shows the figure number (e.g., "Figure 24.12"). The study guide (§9) lists the ones that matter.
7. **Time**: 1.5 min/question budget. You finished in 31 of 75 minutes, so you have room to check notes on anything uncertain.
