Hi Sukessh,
Those are great questions, and I'll do my best to clarify.
1. You should be familiar with the diagrams included in the PowerPoints, as they are there to help illustrate important concepts. Rather than focusing solely on memorizing every label, I recommend understanding what the diagrams represent, how the different structures relate to one another, and any processes they illustrate. You should be able to recognize and identify important structures, but more importantly, understand their functions and significance. Exam questions may ask you to identify structures, interpret diagrams, or apply your understanding of the concepts they illustrate. However, I do tend to use very few pictures and diagrams in online exams, as sometimes they do not load correctly on various devices.
2. You're correct that Zygomycota is no longer recognized as a valid phylum in modern fungal classification. The dashed line in the PowerPoint reflects the fact that this is an older classification that has undergone revisions. For the exam, I would focus on understanding the major fungal groups presented in the PowerPoint, their distinguishing characteristics, and their evolutionary relationships. You should recognize that Zygomycota is a historical grouping rather than a currently accepted phylum, but you do not need to memorize the details of the taxonomic revisions.
3. For this topic, knowing that dinoflagellates can reproduce both sexually and asexually, and understanding the general difference between these two modes of reproduction, is sufficient. You do not need to memorize the detailed reproductive mechanisms described in the textbook.
As I've mentioned before, the PowerPoints should be your primary guide when preparing for the exams. However, remember that the slides are meant to highlight important concepts, not necessarily provide every detail needed to fully understand them. The textbook is a great resource for expanding on those concepts when you need additional clarification.
