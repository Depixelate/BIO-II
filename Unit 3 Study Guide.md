## Slide errors: learn the correct version

She confirmed on Oct 22: when a slide and the textbook disagree, follow the textbook, and ask her about any conflict you notice. Exception (your decision, Oct 28): where the textbook contradicts itself, follow the slide. The printed notes show each correction on its slide, marked "Textbook:"; a figure description too garbled to fix is replaced by the textbook figure's ("Fig (textbook version)"). Slide numbers are the printed chapter.section.slide codes.

| Slide says | Correct version |
|---|---|
| 28.1.4 (figure): "close-up of haploid cells" | Most animals begin life as a **diploid (2n) zygote** (textbook Fig. 28.1d). |
| 28.1.8: radial symmetry = "two identical halves" | Radial: **any** lengthwise cut through the center gives identical halves; bilateral: only **one** cut does (p. 510). |
| 28.1.11: "Protostomes – Spiral cleavage" | Spiral cleavage in **most** protostomes, **not ecdysozoans** (roundworms, arthropods). The first opening is the **blastopore**, formed when the blastula indents (p. 512). |
| 28.2.15: comb jellies "may have been the first organisms to evolve" | Possibly the oldest **animal** lineage (DNA seems older than sponges'), but this is **still debated** (p. 514). |
| 28.2.16: "Inner layer – Gastrovascular cavity" | The inner layer is a **tissue** (from endoderm) that secretes digestive juices **into** the gastrovascular cavity; mesoglea lies between the layers (p. 514). |
| 28.3.22: "Lophophorus", "Protozoans (flatworms, rotifers, molluscs, annelids)" | **Lophophorans** and **trochozoans**. Protozoans are protists. Trochozoans (mollusks, annelids) have a trochophore larva; flatworms and rotifers count as trochozoans by descent (p. 516). |
| 28.3.24: mollusks "100,000 named species" | **Over 150,000**, the second most numerous animal group (p. 516). Low priority (numbers). |
| 28.3.44: flukes "usually hermaphroditic: Schistosoma and Clonorchis" | **Schistosoma has separate sexes** (female flukes lay eggs; adults copulate in the blood vessels, p. 522). |
| 28.4.50: ecdysozoans "have evolved separate sexes" | **Many** ecdysozoans have (p. 525). |
| 28.4.55 (figure): epicuticle, exocuticle, endocuticle as "layers of skin" | Layers of the **cuticle** (exoskeleton): epicuticle waxy, outer; exocuticle hardened by calcium carbonate; endocuticle inner (Fig. 28.31). |
| 28.4.56: crustaceans have "five pairs of appendages" | Five pairs **on the head** (antennae, antennules, mandibles, two pairs of maxillae); more appendages on the thorax and abdomen (p. 527). |
| 28.4.66 (figure): tympanum on the thorax, wings on the abdomen | **Wings (and legs) are on the thorax**; the tympanum is on the **first abdominal segment** (p. 528–529). |
| 28.4.67: "over 1,500 species of scorpions" | The textbook: about 1,500 species **in North America** (p. 531). Low priority. |
| 28.5.72: "Spines from exoskeletal plates" | **Endoskeletal** plates (echinoderms have an endoskeleton, p. 532). |
| 29.1.3: nerve cord "Protected by vertebrae" | Only **in vertebrates**; lancelets and sea squirts have no vertebrae (p. 537). |
| 29.1.8 (figure) | Garbled tree; replaced by the textbook's Fig. 29.4: notochord → vertebrae → jaws → bony skeleton → lungs → 4 limbs → amniotic egg → mammary glands. |
| 29.2.12: "Jawed fishes and all other invertebrates" | **Vertebrates** (gnathostomes = jawed fishes and the other vertebrates, p. 540). |
| 29.3.13 and 29.3.20: 28,000 fishes; 25,000 bony fishes | **Nearly 33,000** fishes; **over 32,000** bony fishes (p. 540, 542). Low priority. |
| 29.3.15 vs 29.3.16: jaws from the "first pair" vs "first and second" gill arches | No conflict: the jaws came from the **first** pair; the **second** pair became the jaws' supports (p. 540–541). |
| 29.4.25 (figure): "some reptiles (birds and mammals)" | Four chambers in **some reptiles (crocodilians and birds) and mammals** (Fig. 29.11). |
| 29.4.28: caecilians "sightless" | **Often** sightless (p. 544). |
| 29.5.34 and 29.6.43 (figures) | Garbled timelines; replaced by the textbook's Figs. 29.16 (synapsid → mammals; anapsid → turtles; diapsid → lizards, snakes, tuataras, crocodilians, dinosaurs, birds) and 29.20 (monotremes first; marsupials and placentals share live birth and a true uterus). |
| 29.6.44: placenta "used for the exchange of maternal and fetal blood" | It exchanges **substances between** the two bloods (nutrients in, wastes out); the bloods don't mix (p. 553). |
| 30.1.11: "Gorillas and orangutans are also hominins" | They are **hominids**. Hominins = humans and their closest extinct relatives (p. 558, 570). |
| 30.2.21 (figure): sediba "1.9 to 2.0", neanderthalensis "0.15 to 0.35", sapiens "0 to 2" | About **2.0**; **0.35 to 0.03**; about **0.2 to the present** (Fig. 30.6; p. 567–568). |
| 30.2.27: australopiths "110 to 115 centimeters" | **100–115 cm** (p. 563). Low priority. |
| 30.2.29: "A. africanus, A. robustus, A. sediba: small brain, long arms, climbed trees; humanlike pelvis, nose, and dentition" | The robust one is **Paranthropus** robustus; the description is **A. sediba's** (p. 564). |
| 30.4.38: H. floresiensis "replaced H. erectus in Africa... first species to build shelters" | Two species run together: **H. floresiensis** is the dwarf island species; the rest describes **H. heidelbergensis** (p. 566). |
| 29.2.12: chordates appear "542 MYA, start of Cambrian" | **Not corrected: follow the slide.** The textbook contradicts itself (p. 323: the Cambrian began about 542 mya; p. 539: "about 485 mya"). |

Typos fixed silently in the printed notes (no change in meaning): "poly body" (polyp), "trophophore" (trochophore), "germovitellariumon", "cray fish", "a pair of four walking legs" (four pairs), "Galeocerdovi cuer" (Galeocerdo cuvier), "Africa Arabia" (Africarabia), "evolved in" (into), "florensiensis" (floresiensis), the missing "fire" in 30.4.47, "anthropods" (anthropoids), "lateral rip" (rib), and the run-together first line of 28.3.33 ("segmented worms: partitions (septa) divide the coelom").

Left as printed, because the textbook has the same: "Actacus fluviatilis" (the genus is usually spelled Astacus), "Afrocoricida" (usually Afrosoricida), Ardi's "55 kg (110 lb)" (55 kg is about 121 lb).
